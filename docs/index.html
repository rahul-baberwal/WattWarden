About the app

A warden for your phone's battery

Phones hide most of what the battery is doing. WattWarden reads the hardware sensors Android exposes and turns them into plain answers: how fast is this charger really charging, is my battery getting old, and is something draining it while I sleep. The shield is the warden; the bolt is the watt.

Features

Everything in one place

Live power dashboard

Real-time wattage gauge, current, voltage and temperature, with estimated time to 80% and 100%.

Charger & cable tester

Logs peak and average watts for each session. Name sessions like "65W charger + blue cable" and compare them side by side.

Thermal monitor

Alerts you when the battery gets hotter than your chosen limit while fast-charging or gaming.

Battery health estimate

Measures charge flow across a charge to estimate real capacity in mAh, shown against the design value.

Charge cycle counter

Partial charges add up: 20% to 70% counts as half a cycle.

Charge-limit alarm

Get notified at 80% (or any limit you set) to reduce lithium-ion wear.

Trickle-charge guard

Warns you if the phone stays pinned at 100% on the charger for too long, for example overnight.

Phantom-drain detector

Measures battery loss per hour with the screen off and alerts you if it is unusually high.

Smart-plug webhooks

Send an HTTP request to Home Assistant, IFTTT or any endpoint to cut power at your limit and restore it at a low level.

Live notification

Ongoing notification with watts, temperature and time estimates, always visible in the shade.

Home-screen widget

Battery level, status, temperature, health and screen-on time left at a glance.

Calibration

Fixes phones that report current in different units or with reversed sign (Auto / µA / mA / invert).

How to use

Up and running in a minute

Allow notifications

Grant the notification permission on first launch so alerts and the live status can appear.

Turn on Guardian

On the Live tab, switch Guardian on. It records charging sessions and watches your limits in the background.

Plug in a charger

The gauge shows live watts, current, voltage and temperature. A charging curve builds as the session runs.

Name your charger

Open History, tap a session and choose Name charger. Rankings then appear on the Health tab.

Measure battery health

Charge across at least a 20% span (for example 30% to 55%) with Guardian on. Health appears after your first qualifying charge and improves over time.

Set your protections

In Settings, choose your charge limit, heat limit, trickle-charge time and drain threshold. Add smart-plug webhook URLs if you want automatic cut-off.

Keep it alive

On some phones (Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Huawei) also allow auto-start and exempt WattWarden from battery optimisation, using the shortcut in Settings.

Tips

If the watts look about 1000× too big or too small, change the unit under Settings → Calibration.

If charging shows as negative, turn on Invert current sign.

Compare chargers by using the same phone state each time (screen off, same starting percentage).

Privacy Policy

Your data stays on your phone

Effective date: 19 November 2026 · App: WattWarden (package com.wattwarden.app) · Developer: Rahul Baberwal

1. Summary

WattWarden does not collect, sell or share your personal data. It has no accounts, no ads and no analytics or tracking tools. Everything the app records stays on your device.

2. Information the app handles

To work, the app reads battery information from Android and stores it locally:

  • Battery level, voltage, current, temperature, charging state and charge counter
  • Charging session history (times, levels, power and temperature readings)
  • Screen-on and screen-off state, used only to calculate battery drain rates
  • Your settings (limits, thresholds, calibration, optional webhook URLs)

This information is stored in the app's private storage on your device. It is not uploaded to us or to any third party. You can remove it at any time with Settings → Clear all history, or by clearing the app's data or uninstalling it.

3. Network use

The app has no servers. It makes a network request only if you enter a smart-plug webhook URL in Settings. In that case it sends a small message (event name, battery level and temperature) to the exact address you entered, such as your Home Assistant or IFTTT endpoint. That service is operated by you or a third party you chose, and is subject to its own privacy policy.

4. Permissions

  • Notifications: to show alerts and the live status notification.
  • Foreground service: to monitor charging continuously while Guardian is on.
  • Internet: only for the webhooks described above.
  • Run at startup: to resume monitoring after the phone restarts.

The app does not request access to your contacts, location, camera, microphone, photos or files.

5. Children

WattWarden is a general utility and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

6. Security

Because data never leaves your device, it is protected by your phone's own security. Keep your device lock enabled and your Android version up to date.

7. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version and a new effective date will be posted on this page.
